what_i_learned: |
  Today, we kicked off the week by making significant progress on both our academic writing and our technical application. First, we focused on writing the results section of our paper, detailing our model's performance and fairness metrics. After wrapping up that writing session, we transitioned back to app development. To elevate our user interface, we researched existing skin lesion screening apps to study their layouts and gather fresh design ideas. Applying what we learned, we redesigned our landing page and the login/signup screens to make them more intuitive and professional. We also spent time integrating the device's camera feature so users can eventually capture images directly through the app. Finally, we deployed a web version of our app to inspect how the layout scales on desktop screens and identify areas for cross-platform improvement.

blockers: |
  While getting the user interface elements aligned went smoothly, implementing the live camera integration was a bit of a headache. Ensuring the camera functions properly within our framework and captures high-enough quality images for our backend model to analyze without lagging or crashing the UI took a lot of trial and error. 

reflection: |
  Today was satisfying because our project is starting to look and feel like a real-world product. Looking at other established medical screening apps gave us a great benchmark for what standard we need to hit. Seeing our login flows and landing pages come together on both mobile and web viewports makes the application feel incredibly polished. It is exciting to see our theoretical research transform into a tangible, accessible tool!.
