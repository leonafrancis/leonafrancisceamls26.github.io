what_i_learned: |
  We wrapped up our deep dive exploration of the new datasets today. For my assignment with the SkinCon dataset, I had to log the shape, class distribution, skin tone coverage, and check for missing values. Through this Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), I made a critical discovery: this specific dataset is actually unhelpful for our project because its classes are completely unrelated to skin cancer, and it lacks any skin tone descriptions. Pivoting quickly, I identified two promising alternatives to explore next week: the SCIN dataset and the MSKCC dataset. Later, we transitioned to a fascinating biology lecture from Dr. Pramanik about DNA architecture. We learned about the foundational components of DNA, including purines (adenine and guanine), pyrimidines (cytosine and thymine), deoxyribose sugars, and how they link together to form nucleotides. I had a good time during this session because it reminded me of my biology class in highschool, which was one of my favorite classes. We ended the day by shifting back to our research paper, and adding in our refrences then combing through the paper and adding in the citations.

blockers: |
  Realizing that a dataset you just spent time downloading, cleaning, and analyzing is ultimately a dead end for your specific research goals can be incredibly frustrating. It felt like a setback to find out SkinCon didn't have the skin cancer classes or the skin tone diversity data we need for our AI model. However, ruling out unhelpful data is a major part of the scientific process, and finding the SCIN and MSKCC datasets gives us a clear path forward. So im not too upset about it. 

reflection: |
  Today captured the highs and lows of doing real data science research. Even though it was disappointing that SkinCon didn't work out, I'm glad I caught the issue early through thorough EDA rather than trying to force irrelevant data into our model. I really appreciated today's lecture by Dr. Pramanik in reminding us about the biological basis for all of the data we analyze each and every day. Plus, getting our citations properly formatted in Overleaf makes the whole project feel even more official and real since we are heading in to our last few weeks. I'm ready to enjoy the weekend and start working with the new datasets next week!
