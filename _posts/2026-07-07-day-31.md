  Today I started the morning on a high note by diving back into the Google Machine Learning course I started yesterday and successfully finishing it! With that out of the way, my group and I channeled all of our focus back into our project's biggest hurdle: calculating the Individual Typology Angle (ITA) for our images. We actually managed to write the script and complete the baseline implementation to map the ITA values to the Fitzpatrick skin tone scale, which felt like a step in the right direction.

blockers: |
  Even though we finished writing the code, the pipeline is still not working properly. The ITA calculations are misclassifying the skin tones in the images, pairing them with the wrong Fitzpatrick scale types. Frustrated by the inaccuracy, we decided to pivot and test our ITA logic on an entirely different dataset. This threw us into a second roadblock: finding a brand-new, high-quality skin image dataset that we haven't already used or thoroughly reviewed is proving to be incredibly difficult. We spent our final two hours grinding away at this assignment, but we were completely unsuccessful and had to stop without a fix.

reflection: |
  Today was a classic example of "one step forward, two steps back." Finishing my machine learning course felt great, but the frustration of watching our code output inaccurate skin tone classifications definitely put a damper on the afternoon. It is tough when you spend hours debugging only to walk away with the problem still unsolved. However, data science is all about trial and error. We are going to step away from the code for the night, let our brains rest, and pick it back up later this week with a fresh perspective!
